The market you'd enter

Everything above is the terrain. This is the competition — a consolidating, well-funded RegTech market. The strategic read from the modules: this is not an empty space. Compete in the data layer, through a services-led, channel-distributed entry to the under-served mid-market — not as "another ESG dashboard." Tags below mark each as a competitive threat, a potential partner / integration point, or fixed terrain.

The venture's position

Own the data layer. Never the signature.

Sit above iSBEM (let accredited assessors keep the regulated calculation and the legal sign-off) and below the incumbent platforms — in the workflow that gathers a building's data once and reports it to all four pressures. The EPC is the atomic data point; whoever owns the workflow around it sits at the centre.

The wedge

Audit-grade building data, gathered once, reported to regulator, investor, lender and tenant.

The boundary

Track, forecast and supply data for the assessor — never "we improve your EPC." Only they can.

The segment

The vulnerable mid-market — the 85–90% the big platforms move up-market and away from.

The route

Services-led, channel-distributed (agents, accountants). Earn trust first, productise underneath.

Geographic origin

UK — bites every UK owner. MEES has legal teeth; the rest are disclosure/reporting burdens.
EU — only bites a UK owner with EU exposure (subsidiary, operations, exports). CSRD narrowed by the 2026 Omnibus.
Global — not laws but standards: either adopted into UK rules (ISSB → UKSRS) or demanded by international capital (GRESB, LEED).

How to read the stack

Layers 1→7 read top-down: rules are set, instruments issued, gatekeepers license, data is created, benchmarks score it, four pressures demand it, the owner absorbs it.
Layer 4 (purple) is where building data is born — the layer the iSBEM guides illuminate, and the layer the venture targets.
The EPC node is the most reused data point in the whole system — the hub everything else consumes.
