1. What UKSRS actually is, in one paragraph

The UK Sustainability Reporting Standards (UKSRS or "UK SRS") are the UK's official, government-endorsed standards for how companies should disclose sustainability and climate-related information to investors and capital markets. They consist of two documents — UK SRS S1 (general sustainability disclosures) and UK SRS S2 (climate-specific disclosures) — and they were finalised and published on 25 February 2026 by the Department for Business and Trade (DBT). They are currently voluntary. The Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) is consulting on making them mandatory for listed companies from 1 January 2027.

Read that paragraph again. Every word matters. Now let's unpack each piece.

2. The lineage — where UKSRS comes from

UKSRS doesn't exist in a vacuum. To understand it, you need to understand the lineage:

2015  TCFD (Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures) created by FSB
      → produces voluntary climate disclosure framework
      → becomes de facto global standard
      
2021  TCFD becomes MANDATORY for UK premium-listed companies (and large private)

2023  June: ISSB (International Sustainability Standards Board) publishes
            IFRS S1 + IFRS S2 — the new global baseline
      October: TCFD formally DISBANDED. Its work absorbed into ISSB.
      
2024  ISSB standards begin being adopted by 30+ jurisdictions

2025  June–Sept: UK government consults on adopting ISSB as UK SRS

2026  25 Feb: UK SRS S1 and S2 FINALISED. Voluntary.
      30 Jan: FCA consultation (CP26/5) on making them mandatory
              for listed companies. Consultation closed 20 March 2026.
      Autumn (expected): FCA publishes final policy statement
      
2027  1 January (expected): Mandatory reporting begins for listed companies

The key insight: UKSRS is the UK's national adoption of the ISSB global baseline. The UK didn't invent something new — it endorsed the international standard, with six minor UK-specific tweaks. This matters because:

  • A UK company complying with UKSRS is largely complying with the same standard used in 30+ other jurisdictions
  • Investors get globally comparable data
  • The UK signals it's a "world-leading sustainable finance jurisdiction" (this is explicit government rhetoric)
  • The shift from TCFD to UKSRS is evolutionary, not revolutionary — TCFD's four pillars (governance, strategy, risk management, metrics & targets) are preserved inside UKSRS

Why your co-founder pointed at UKSRS specifically: It's the newest piece of regulation in the stack. It's the one where workflows haven't yet been built, software hasn't yet been productized, and consultancies are still figuring out their offerings. That's the venture-creation window.

3. UK SRS S1 vs UK SRS S2 — what each one does

The two standards do different things. Memorize this distinction.

UK SRS S1 — General Requirements

Full title: "General Requirements for Disclosure of Sustainability-related Financial Information"

What it covers: All sustainability topics that could be financially material to the company — climate, biodiversity, water, workforce, supply chain, governance, social impact, whatever is material to the specific entity.

The core requirement: Disclose any sustainability-related risk or opportunity that "could reasonably be expected to affect the entity's cash flows, access to finance, or cost of capital over the short, medium, or long term."

The four-pillar structure (inherited from TCFD):

  1. Governance — How the board and management oversee sustainability risks and opportunities
  2. Strategy — How sustainability affects business model, strategy, and financial planning
  3. Risk Management — How sustainability risks are identified, assessed, and managed
  4. Metrics and Targets — What KPIs are used to measure and track performance

S1 is essentially the framework — the rules for how to do disclosure in general.

UK SRS S2 — Climate-related Disclosures

Full title: "Climate-related Disclosures"

What it covers: The same four-pillar structure as S1, but specifically applied to climate-related risks and opportunities.

What S2 adds on top of S1:

  • Specific climate scenario analysis requirements
  • Quantitative metrics — GHG emissions across Scopes 1, 2, and 3, climate transition risks
  • Climate-related capital deployment
  • Industry-specific metrics (e.g., for real estate, banking, transport)
  • Targets aligned with the company's climate-related transition

The relationship between S1 and S2 (this is important):

S1 is the general framework. S2 is a specific application of that framework to climate. If you report under S2 only, you can still claim compliance with UKSRS S2 — but you cannot claim compliance with S1 unless you address all material sustainability topics, not just climate.

Most companies will start with S2 (climate-only) and expand to S1 (full sustainability) over time. The UK explicitly permits this phasing.

4. The six UK-specific tweaks to IFRS

This is the technical detail you'll need to be able to discuss with a sustainability professional. UKSRS is very close to IFRS S1/S2 but has six small differences. Knowing these makes you sound like an insider.

#UK-Specific AmendmentWhy it matters
1Removal of "delayed reporting" reliefIFRS S1 lets companies publish sustainability info later than financial statements in year 1. UKSRS removed this — sustainability and financial reports must be published together. Increases data-prep urgency.
2Extension of "climate-first" relief from 1 year to 2 yearsCompanies can report climate-only (S2) in years 1 and 2, then expand to all sustainability topics (S1) in year 3. Gives more time for non-climate data.
3SASB references made permissive, not mandatoryIFRS requires considering Sustainability Accounting Standards Board industry guidance. UKSRS makes this optional — the US-centric SASB doesn't fit UK contexts well.
4Transition reliefs linked to mandatory dateVoluntary early adopters don't get penalised — reliefs apply from when reporting becomes mandatory, not from when you start voluntarily reporting.
5Removed timing references for Scope 3 reliefCompanies can defer Scope 3 emissions reporting indefinitely (provided they explain why), until regulators specify a deadline. Big practical relief — Scope 3 is the hardest data.
6Subordination to UK lawUKSRS sits underneath Companies Act 2006, FCA rules, and other UK regulation. Where there's conflict, UK law wins.

Why this matters for your venture: Software/consulting that helps UK companies comply with UKSRS needs to handle UK-specific reliefs correctly. A "global ISSB" platform that doesn't know about UK Amendment 5 (Scope 3 relief) will get UK customers in trouble.

5. Who UKSRS applies to (scope)

This is where it gets practically important. Right now, UKSRS is voluntary for everyone. But the mandatory pathway is being defined in real-time:

The mandatory pathway (proposed, not yet final)

FCA's CP26/5 consultation (closed 20 March 2026) proposes mandatory UKSRS reporting for:

  • UK Premium Listed companies — first wave from 1 January 2027
  • UK Standard Listed companies — likely second wave
  • Large private companies — being consulted on separately by DBT (no firm date)
  • Asset managers and AIFs — separate FCA rules under consideration

The FCA is expected to publish final rules in Autumn 2026, taking effect from 1 January 2027 for reporting periods starting that date or later. So first UKSRS-mandatory annual reports will be filed in early 2028 (for FY27).

Why everyone should be preparing now

Even companies not yet in scope are preparing because:

  • Investor pressure — large investors are asking portfolio companies to start UKSRS-aligned reporting voluntarily
  • GRESB alignment — GRESB is updating its framework to map to ISSB/UKSRS
  • Lender pressure — green loan and SLL covenants increasingly reference UKSRS-style metrics
  • Supply chain — large companies need Scope 3 data from suppliers, which forces those suppliers to start measuring even if they're not directly in scope

For your venture: the voluntary period is actually the gold rush window. Companies start preparing now to avoid 2027 scramble. Tools and services that get traction now lock in customers before mandatory deadlines.

6. What UKSRS actually requires you to disclose (the substance)

Let me walk through what a UKSRS-compliant disclosure looks like in practice. Pretend you're a UK REIT preparing your FY27 annual report under UKSRS S2 (climate).

Governance disclosures

  • Who on the board has climate oversight responsibility (named individuals/committees)
  • How often climate issues are discussed at board level
  • How management roles, processes, and controls are structured for climate
  • How climate is integrated into executive remuneration

Strategy disclosures

  • Climate-related risks and opportunities identified
  • Time horizons for assessment (short, medium, long term — and define what those mean for you)
  • Effects on business model and strategy — qualitative and quantitative
  • Effects on financial position — current and anticipated
  • Climate resilience — scenario analysis showing how the business performs under different climate futures (typically 1.5°C, 2°C, and 3°C+ scenarios)
  • Transition plan — if you have one, disclose it

Risk management disclosures

  • Process for identifying climate risks
  • How those risks are prioritized
  • How they integrate with the enterprise risk management framework

Metrics and targets disclosures

  • GHG emissions:
  • Scope 1 (direct emissions from owned/controlled sources)
  • Scope 2 (indirect emissions from purchased energy)
  • Scope 3 (all other value-chain emissions — relief available in UK)
  • Other metrics:
  • Climate-related transition risks (e.g., assets vulnerable to carbon pricing)
  • Climate-related physical risks (e.g., flood-exposed assets)
  • Climate-related opportunities (e.g., revenue from green products)
  • Capital deployment to climate-related activities
  • Internal carbon prices used
  • Remuneration linked to climate
  • Targets:
  • Net-zero or other climate targets
  • Interim targets
  • Use of carbon credits

Industry-specific requirements (for real estate)

UKSRS S2 includes industry-specific guidance (drawn from SASB/ISSB Industry Standards). For Real Estate, key metrics include:

  • Total energy consumption (by source and property type)
  • Total water withdrawal
  • Like-for-like energy intensity (kWh/m²) by property type
  • % of portfolio with green building certifications
  • % of leases with green clauses
  • Operational emissions per m² by property type
  • Embodied carbon in development activity
  • Climate physical risk exposure of the portfolio
  • Transition risk: % of portfolio at risk of stranding under MEES tightening

This is where it directly meets MEES, GRESB, and the broader stack. UKSRS forces the REIT to disclose, in its audited annual report, the very data points that MEES will judge them on.

The relationships:

  • UKSRS is the disclosure layer. It says "tell investors about your climate risk."
  • MEES, EPCs, SECR, ESOS are the underlying compliance regimes that produce the data UKSRS asks you to disclose.
  • GRESB sits parallel — same data, different audience (private investors rather than public capital markets).
  • Green loans and SLLs use the same data to define their covenants.

This means: the same data set, gathered once, must be reported in multiple formats to multiple audiences. One data source, many disclosures. This is one of the cleanest venture wedges in the space.

8. UKSRS vs CSRD — the EU comparison

You will be asked about this constantly. UKSRS and CSRD (EU's Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive) are not the same thing.

DimensionUKSRSEU CSRD
MaterialitySingle (financial) — "what affects the business"Double — "what affects the business" AND "what the business affects"
StandardsUK SRS S1 + S2 (ISSB-based)ESRS (European Sustainability Reporting Standards) — 12 topical standards
ScopeInvestor-focused disclosureWider — investors, society, regulators
GranularityPrinciples-based, relatively flexibleHighly prescriptive, ~1,100+ data points
AssuranceLimited (audit standards still being defined)Mandatory limited assurance, moving to reasonable
PhasingVoluntary now, mandatory expected 2027Active in waves since 2024
Scope 3Relief available indefinitely in UKRequired from year 2
UK companies affected by CSRD?N/AYes — UK companies with EU operations, large EU subsidiaries, or €150m+ EU turnover

The practical implication: A UK REIT with significant EU operations may need to file both UKSRS disclosures (for UK investors) and CSRD disclosures (for EU jurisdictions where it operates). The data overlaps, but the formats don't.

This is a venture opportunity in its own right. Tools that handle UKSRS + CSRD + GRESB from a single underlying data model are valuable to dual-listed or cross-border firms.

9. The five hardest parts of UKSRS compliance (where pain lives)

This is the part most relevant for venture thinking. Where do companies actually struggle?

Pain Point 1: Scope 3 emissions data

Scope 3 includes all value-chain emissions — purchased goods, downstream use, employee commuting, financed emissions, tenant emissions if you're a landlord. For UK REITs, Scope 3 includes the emissions of the tenants in their buildings (categorised as "downstream leased assets").

  • The data lives with the tenant, not the landlord
  • Tenants may not measure it, may not share it, or may share it in inconsistent formats
  • The UK Scope 3 relief helps, but doesn't solve the long-term problem
  • This is the data-gathering challenge for UK CRE under UKSRS

Why it's a venture opportunity: Anyone who can broker, structure, and verify Scope 3 data flows between landlords and tenants has a defensible position.

Pain Point 2: Scenario analysis

UKSRS requires companies to analyze how their business performs under different climate scenarios (1.5°C, 2°C, 3°C+). This requires:

  • Mapping all assets to physical locations
  • Modeling physical risks (flood, heat, storm) at each location
  • Modeling transition risks (carbon pricing, regulation, market shifts)
  • Quantifying financial impact under each scenario

For a REIT with hundreds of buildings, this is a multi-month exercise typically outsourced to consultancies like Munich Re, Jupiter Intelligence, or specialist climate-risk firms — none of which are cheap.

Pain Point 3: Connectivity with financial statements

UKSRS (per UK Amendment 1) requires sustainability and financial reporting to be published simultaneously. This sounds simple but isn't. It means:

  • Sustainability data must be audit-ready at the same time as financials
  • Both must use consistent boundaries (consolidation methods, reporting periods)
  • Both must reflect the same risks (financial impact of climate must show in both)

Most companies have these systems totally siloed today. Bringing them together is a multi-year change project.

Pain Point 4: Forward-looking targets and transition plans

UKSRS requires disclosure of climate targets and (if you have one) your transition plan. Setting these requires:

  • A credible 1.5°C-aligned net-zero pathway
  • Interim targets (typically 2030, 2040)
  • Detailed asset-level retrofit and decarbonisation plans
  • Carbon budget management

For a REIT, this means modelling the retrofit pathway for every building in the portfolio — capex, sequencing, scope, dependencies. This is what JLL, EVORA, and others charge huge fees to do.

Pain Point 5: Assurance and audit readiness

UKSRS hasn't yet specified assurance requirements, but the trend (driven by CSRD and FCA expectations) is clear: limited assurance moving to reasonable assurance over time. This means:

  • Big Four auditors (PwC, KPMG, EY, Deloitte) build audit trails on sustainability data
  • All inputs must be traceable, version-controlled, and methodologically consistent
  • Manual spreadsheets and PowerPoints don't survive audit

This is the single biggest force pulling the industry toward proper software platforms. Spreadsheets can't be audited at the scale UKSRS demands.

10. What's NOT in UKSRS (the limits)

Equally important — what UKSRS does not require:

  • No "double materiality." UK uses single (financial) materiality. You don't have to disclose impacts that don't financially matter to your business. (CSRD does require this.)
  • No specific climate target. UKSRS requires you to disclose targets if you have them. It doesn't require net-zero by 2050.
  • No sector-specific quantitative thresholds. Unlike CSRD's ESRS standards, UKSRS doesn't have prescriptive sector-level numerics.
  • No mandatory third-party assurance yet. Coming, but not specified.
  • No application to small unlisted private companies (yet). Even when mandatory, the initial scope is listed companies.

This relative flexibility is intentional — the UK is positioning UKSRS as "principles-based" vs CSRD's "rules-based" approach. It's friendlier to companies but creates more variation in disclosures, which itself is a software/consulting opportunity (helping companies benchmark against peers).

12. The competitive dynamics already forming

In the three months since UKSRS finalisation (Feb 2026), the following has already happened:

  • Big consulting firms (PwC, KPMG, EY, Deloitte, plus JLL, CBRE, Savills) have all launched UKSRS readiness assessments and disclosure-drafting services
  • Existing ESG software platforms (Workiva, Diligent, Watershed, Sweep, Persefoni, Plan A) are positioning themselves as UKSRS-ready
  • Specialist UK CRE platforms (Deepki, Measurabl, Sustainable Platform, EVORA's SIERA) are emphasising their UKSRS reporting modules
  • The Big Four audit firms are quietly preparing their assurance methodologies for when assurance becomes mandatory

What's NOT yet built (gaps you might exploit):

  • Specialist UK CRE-focused UKSRS tooling for mid-market landlords (the big platforms target tier-1 REITs)
  • Tools that bridge tenant↔landlord Scope 3 data flows for green-leased buildings
  • Audit-trail-first platforms designed for assurance readiness from day one
  • Affordable, opinionated, productized solutions vs the current "platform with consulting" model

We'll cover this in much more depth in Module 7 (Competitive Landscape). For now, internalize: the field isn't empty, but it isn't crowded either, and crucially no incumbent has yet won the UK CRE UKSRS-specific category.

13. Honest caveats

A few things to flag openly:

1. The mandatory date isn't actually final yet. FCA Autumn 2026 policy statement is when 1 Jan 2027 becomes real. There's still a small chance of slippage.

2. The voluntary nature creates a strange market. Companies adopting now do so for investor signalling, not regulatory necessity. Their willingness to pay for sophisticated tools is real but uneven.

3. UKSRS is "principles-based" — interpretation matters. Two companies can both claim UKSRS compliance with very different disclosures. This creates demand for benchmarking, but also makes "compliance" hard to define crisply.

4. The Big Four moat is real. When assurance becomes mandatory, PwC/KPMG/EY/Deloitte will own the audit relationships and may bundle technology with audit, squeezing out independent software vendors. Timing your venture matters.

5. We're modelling from public sources. What we don't yet know is what actual UK REIT sustainability teams are experiencing day-to-day. The next module after this should probably be buyer-interview-driven, even if just from secondary sources.
