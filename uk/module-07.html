Where we are: Modules 1–6 built the world, the rules, and the buyers. This is the last input before synthesis. A venture's success depends less on whether a market exists (it clearly does) and more on whether there's a defensible gap between the incumbents. This module maps who's already selling to Priya, James, and Sarah — and, crucially, where the seams are that a tech-strong, bootstrapped, India-based team could slip through. Reading time: ~25 minutes.

1. How to think about competition here (the framework)

Don't think of competitors as a flat list. Think of them as bands, arranged by what they fundamentally are, because each band competes differently, prices differently, and is vulnerable differently:

   BAND 1 — THE PURE-PLAY REAL ESTATE ESG PLATFORMS
   (software-first, built specifically for property)
   Deepki · Measurabl · EVORA SIERA · Enertiv · BuildingMinds

   BAND 2 — THE HORIZONTAL ESG / CARBON PLATFORMS
   (not real-estate-specific, but land in the space)
   Watershed · Persefoni · Sweep · Plan A · Workiva · Diligent

   BAND 3 — THE CONSULTANCIES (advisory-led, some with tooling)
   JLL · CBRE · Savills (big agents) · EVORA · Longevity ·
   Carbon Intelligence (Accenture) · the Big Four

   BAND 4 — THE ADJACENT / INFRASTRUCTURE TOOLS
   (touch the data but aren't ESG-reporting plays)
   Yardi · MRI (property management systems) ·
   BMS / metering / submetering vendors · SkenarioLabs

   BAND 5 — THE STANDARD-SETTERS / DATA OWNERS
   (not vendors, but shape the field)
   GRESB · BREEAM/BRE · the EPC register

Your venture will be compared against Band 1 most directly, will bump into Band 3 in every sales process, and will need to decide whether Bands 4 and 5 are partners or threats. Let's go through each.

2. Band 1 — The pure-play real estate ESG platforms (your direct comparators)

These are the companies a buyer will name-check when you pitch. "How are you different from Deepki?" is a question you must be able to answer in one sentence.

Deepki

  • Origin: Paris, 2014. The category leader by revenue and scale.
  • What it is: A "fully populated ESG data intelligence platform combined with expert advisory services" — i.e. software plus consulting. Holds GRESB Assessment Partner status.
  • Scale: 600+ customers, monitors roughly €4 trillion in assets across 80+ countries. Raised $167M+ (Series C in 2022). Acquired UK firm Fabriq in 2022 to build energy-efficiency capability and UK reach.
  • Strength: Huge automated data-connector layer (7,300+ connectors), four-layer data validation, benchmarking against a massive dataset. Holds ISAE 3000 / SOC 2 audit credentials.
  • Who they sell to: Tier-1 — Generali RE, PGIM, Swiss Life, large institutional owners.

Measurabl

  • Origin: San Diego, 2013. The other global category leader; arguably the most widely adopted.
  • What it is: "World's most widely adopted ESG data platform" for real estate. ~18 billion sq ft across 93 countries, $3T+ in assets, ~37% of the world's top asset managers. Raised $93M Series D (2023).
  • Strength: Data aggregation, benchmarking, GRESB/ENERGY STAR reporting, integrated physical climate-risk data. New "Quantum" data cloud and "Navigate" net-zero pathway product.
  • Who they sell to: Large owners, operators, investors — again tier-1.

EVORA Global (SIERA)

  • Origin: London, 2011. The most UK-centric of the big three.
  • What it is: A consultancy-first business with its own software platform (SIERA). This is the "consulting + tool" model in its purest form — and the closest analogue to the consulting-led path you're considering.
  • Strength: Deep UK CRE domain expertise, GRESB advisory, strong relationships with UK funds. SIERA is the data/reporting layer beneath their advisory work.
  • Who they sell to: UK and European funds and REITs.

The others in Band 1

  • Enertiv — vertically integrated, strong on asset-level equipment/BMS monitoring (not just portfolio reporting). US-centric.
  • BuildingMinds — German (Schindler-backed), digital-twin-flavoured portfolio platform.
  • Accuracy/newer entrants — Tracxn counts 44+ ESG-in-real-estate startups, 27 funded.

The Band 1 pattern you must internalise:

Every serious Band 1 player has converged on the same shape: a portfolio-level ESG data platform that ingests building data, benchmarks it, and outputs GRESB + framework reports — bundled with advisory services. They are large, well-funded, tier-1-focused, and increasingly similar to each other.

3. Band 2 — The horizontal ESG/carbon platforms

These weren't built for real estate, but they show up in deals — especially when the buyer is a large corporate that thinks of property as just one part of its footprint.

  • Watershed, Persefoni, Plan A, Sweep — enterprise carbon-accounting platforms. Strong on Scope 1/2/3 corporate carbon, weak on building-level real estate specifics (EPC, MEES, GRESB).
  • Workiva, Diligent — disclosure/reporting and GRC (governance, risk, compliance) platforms. Workiva especially is strong on the financial-report-connectivity angle UKSRS demands (remember Module 3, Pain Point 3) — but it's generic, not CRE-aware.

Why they matter to you: They're competitors for the disclosure/reporting layer but blind to the building/asset layer. A CRE owner using Workiva for disclosure still has the building-data problem unsolved. That seam — between generic disclosure tools and building-level reality — is a potential wedge.

4. Band 3 — The consultancies (your shadow competitor in every deal)

This is the band a tech founder most often underestimates, and it's the most important to understand — because in UK CRE, consultancies, not software, are the default incumbent.

The big property agents (JLL, CBRE, Savills, Knight Frank, Cushman & Wakefield)

  • All have substantial sustainability advisory arms.
  • They already hold the client relationship (they manage the buildings, do the valuations, broker the deals).
  • They increasingly bundle ESG advisory into existing mandates.
  • They are simultaneously your biggest competitor AND your best potential channel partner (Module 6's outsourcing wrinkle). They have relationships you can't easily get; you have focus and tooling they're often too big to build well.

The specialist sustainability consultancies

  • EVORA (also Band 1 via SIERA), Longevity Partners, Carbon Intelligence (acquired by Accenture, 2023).
  • They sell expertise by the day: GRESB submissions, net-zero strategy, retrofit pathways, scenario analysis.
  • High margins for them, high cost for the client, not scalable.

The Big Four (PwC, KPMG, EY, Deloitte)

  • Moving aggressively into UKSRS readiness and (eventually) assurance.
  • The looming threat: when UKSRS assurance becomes mandatory, the Big Four own the audit relationship and may bundle technology with audit — potentially squeezing independent vendors (Module 3, caveat 4).

The crucial competitive insight about Band 3:

Consultancies don't sell software; they sell outcomes and trust. A UK CRE owner doesn't wake up wanting "an ESG platform" — they wake up wanting "GRESB done" or "UKSRS handled." The consultancy says "we'll handle it." That's a more natural sale than "buy our tool and do it yourself." This is exactly why the consulting-led entry (Module 6, Section 9) is the right wedge for you — you compete with consultancies on their own terms, then quietly productise underneath.

5. Band 4 — Adjacent and infrastructure tools

  • Yardi, MRI — the dominant property-management/accounting systems. They hold operational and financial building data. They're adding ESG modules. Threat: they own the system of record. Opportunity: their ESG modules are usually weak afterthoughts, and they're not UK-regulation-specific.
  • BMS / metering / submetering vendors — hold the raw energy data. Potential data-source partners.
  • SkenarioLabs — predictive analytics for asset-level risk and value, including ESG/stranding. A more specialised adjacent player.

Why they matter: These tools sit closer to the raw data than the ESG platforms do. Whoever controls the data pipe has leverage. If you build in the data layer, these are your integration points (and possibly your partners or acquirers).

6. Band 5 — The standard-setters

  • GRESB — not a vendor, but it defines the annual assessment everyone reports to. It shapes what data matters. Reporting to GRESB is a recurring pain you could ease.
  • BRE (BREEAM) — owns the dominant UK certification and the SBEM methodology lineage.
  • The EPC register — the open(ish) national dataset of ~1.5M+ non-domestic EPCs. A data asset anyone can build on.

These aren't competitors; they're the terrain. But understanding them reveals data sources and integration points others overlook.

7. The market's direction of travel: consolidation toward "one-stop-shop"

A critical strategic signal: Verdantix (the analyst house that covers this space) explicitly predicts continued consolidation — vendors acquiring each other, the market demanding all-in-one solutions covering reporting + energy efficiency + decarbonisation. Deepki buying Fabriq (2022) is one example; Accenture buying Carbon Intelligence (2023) is another.

What consolidation means for your venture (two-edged):

  • Threat: The big platforms are racing to become comprehensive. A narrow point solution risks being subsumed or out-featured. Building "yet another portfolio ESG dashboard" walks straight into the consolidation buzzsaw.
  • Opportunity: Consolidation creates two openings:
  • The acquisition target play — build something valuable and focused (e.g. a great Scope 3 / tenant-data engine) that a Deepki or Measurabl would rather buy than build. Fabriq did exactly this.
  • The underserved-segment play — as the big players chase ever-larger tier-1 clients with ever-more-comprehensive (and expensive) platforms, they move up-market, abandoning the mid-market. (Module 6's recurring gap.)

8. The competitive map: where are the seams?

Now let's synthesise. Mapping the bands against the buyer personas (Module 6) and the regulatory stack (Modules 3–5) reveals where coverage is thin. Here's the honest assessment of gaps:

Seam 1: The mid-market is genuinely underserved

The big platforms target tier-1 (hundreds of buildings, big budgets). The mid-market landlord (Module 6's "Priya at a mid-size REIT," plus smaller funds and private property companies) is too big for spreadsheets, too small/cost-conscious for a £50k+ Deepki deployment. This gap is real and keeps reappearing. But (Module 6 caveat) underserved ≠ automatically profitable — mid-market pays less and is harder to reach.

Seam 2: Landlord↔tenant Scope 3 data exchange

Nobody has cleanly solved getting tenant energy/emissions data to landlords for UKSRS Scope 3 / green-lease reporting (Module 3, Pain Point 1). The big platforms assume the data exists; the hard part is getting it from the tenant. This is a defensible, specific, painful problem.

Seam 3: UKSRS-specific tooling for CRE doesn't really exist yet

My research found no dedicated "UKSRS reporting tool for real estate" category — just generic GRC/compliance tools and the big platforms bolting on "UKSRS-ready" messaging. UKSRS is four months old. The workflow isn't productised. This is the freshest, most open seam — and it's exactly the one your boss intuited.

Seam 4: Audit-trail-first / assurance-ready design

As assurance looms (Module 3, Pain Point 5), most incumbents bolt audit trails on as an afterthought. A platform designed from the ground up for assurance-readiness (every number traceable, versioned, methodologically documented) addresses a need that's about to become acute.

Seam 5: MEES retrofit-pathway intelligence for the broad market

MEES is the "great leveller" (Module 5) — it hits every building. But sophisticated retrofit-pathway modelling is expensive consultancy work (JLL, EVORA). A cheaper, productised "where do my buildings sit vs the MEES cliff, and what's the cheapest compliance path" tool could serve a far broader market than the consultancies bother with.

9. The honest competitive reality check

Before you get excited about seams, sit with the hard truths:

1. This is not an empty market. It's a consolidating, well-funded market with category leaders who have hundreds of millions in capital, thousands of customers, and years of data accumulation. Anyone who tells you "there's no competition" hasn't looked.

2. Data moats are real. Deepki and Measurabl have ingested data from billions of square feet. Their benchmarking ("how does my building compare to peers?") gets better with scale in a way a new entrant can't easily match.

3. Trust and longevity are gatekeepers (Module 6). A UK fund handing its ESG data to an unknown India-based startup is a hard sell versus a SOC-2-certified platform with Generali as a reference client.

4. The consultancy default is sticky. Many buyers would rather pay a consultant to "handle it" than adopt a tool and do it themselves. You're not just competing with software; you're competing with the desire not to do the work at all.

5. The Big Four assurance bundle could reshape everything. If PwC/KPMG/EY/Deloitte bundle UKSRS tooling with mandatory assurance, the independent-vendor window could narrow fast.

None of this means don't enter. It means enter with clear eyes, through a specific seam, with a defensible angle — not as "another ESG platform."

10. Where a tech-strong, bootstrapped, India-based team realistically fits

Let me connect the competitive picture directly to your specific shape (from our very first conversation):

Your attributeCompetitive implication
Tech/data strongCompete in the data layer (Seam 2, 4), not the relationship layer. Your edge is engineering, not UK property relationships.
India-based, cost-advantagedYou can serve the mid-market (Seam 1) profitably at price points the big platforms can't. Cost arbitrage is a real weapon for under-served, price-sensitive segments.
Bootstrapped / consulting-ledThe consulting-led entry (compete with Band 3 on outcomes) generates cash AND builds the trust/relationships you otherwise lack. Productise underneath over time.
Starting from zero domain knowledgeThis is your real risk. The incumbents have deep domain teams. You're closing that gap (these modules) but must respect it — partner with or hire UK domain expertise before you sell.
Newest regulation (UKSRS) as entrySeam 3 — the freshest, least-productised opening. Being early to UKSRS-for-CRE specifically is your timing advantage.

The shape that emerges (and we'll formalise it in Module 8): Enter consulting-led around the newest, least-served pain (UKSRS readiness + Scope 3/tenant data + MEES exposure) for the mid-market, learn the workflow intimately, and productise the data-layer pieces that software does better than consultants — while treating the big platforms as potential future partners/acquirers, not just rivals.

That's a hypothesis, not a conclusion. Module 8 stress-tests it.

12. Honest caveats

1. I don't have pricing precision. The big platforms don't publish prices (enterprise sales, "contact us"). My "£50k+" figures are informed estimates from market signals, not confirmed quotes. Before any business model assumes price points, this needs validation.

2. The startup count moves fast. "44 startups, 27 funded" (Tracxn, Jan 2026) is a snapshot. New entrants appear constantly; some listed here may have pivoted, merged, or died by the time you read this.

3. I'm assessing seams from the outside. "No UKSRS-for-CRE tool exists yet" is based on search results and absence of evidence — not a guarantee. Someone may be building one in stealth right now. Absence of evidence isn't evidence of absence.

4. The acquisition-target play is seductive but risky. "Build something a big player wants to buy" sounds clever but depends on factors outside your control (their strategy, market timing, your defensibility). Don't build for an exit; build something valuable and let the exit be optional.

5. Competitive landscapes reward primary research. Everything here is desk research. The highest-value validation: talk to someone who has actually bought (or rejected) Deepki/Measurabl/EVORA, and find out why. That single conversation would be worth more than this entire module for de-risking the venture.
