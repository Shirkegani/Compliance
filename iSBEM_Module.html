01

What is actually what

People say "iSBEM" loosely. It's really four nested things: a method, an engine, two modules, and an interface. Knowing which is which tells you what is regulated (and untouchable) versus what is just a workflow (and contestable).

The method
NCM — National Calculation Methodology

MHCLG's official method for non-domestic building energy. Supplies fixed, standardised assumptions via four internal databases — the reason every rating is modelled, not measured.

Activity DBConstruction DBGlazing DBWeather DB
The engine
SBEM — Simplified Building Energy Model

BRE's C++ engine implementing the NCM. Runs two modules:

BRUKL
Part L compliance checker (the BRUKL report).
EPCgen
EPC asset-rating + recommendations generator.
iSBEM — the interface
Free, Windows-only MS Access desktop app. No cloud, no API, local .nct files, version-locked. The default tool most assessors use — and the layer you sit above, not against.

Three things it is not

Not domestic Dwellings use SAP / RdSAP, an entirely separate methodology — not SBEM.

Not a design tool It's a compliance/certification tool. It won't even calculate internal temperatures.

Not the judge No tool can "pass" or "fail" a building. The software only produces information; Building Control decides.

The regulated objects are the method, the engine and the signature. The interface and the data around it are not. That boundary is the whole opportunity map.

03

The assessor's workflow

Four steps. The expensive, unautomatable difficulty lives before the software opens — in gathering and judging data. The software part is almost clerical by comparison.

STEP 1 · the hard part
Gather & judge

Chase drawings that may not exist; physically survey plant rooms & ceiling voids; interrogate contractors; where data is missing, infer fabric from build-date + observation (erring pessimistic). Plus skilled zoning.

STEP 2
Enter the model

Type the building into iSBEM's five forms, in a fixed order (below).

STEP 3
Run

Ratings form → SBEM + EPCgen compute the rating and write all output files.

STEP 4
Review & lodge

Edit/curate recommendations, record the audit trail, then lodge the XML on the National Register.

General · project + certifier ID→ Project DB · constructions + glazing (U-values)→ Geometry · zones → envelopes → openings→ Building Services · HVAC, HWS, PV…→ Ratings · run
The defensible problem isn't running SBEM — it's assembling and standing behind the data SBEM eats. Software can't survey a plant room.

Every override of a default carries an EPC Audit entry — walk-through, survey, technical inspection, as-built docs. The tool already structures evidence provenance; it just buries it in a local .nct, queryable by no one.
05

Two jobs, one model — and the maths

The same building model answers two different questions against two different theoretical benchmarks. To do it, SBEM secretly builds three buildings (every object ×3), plus a fourth derived as a stock average.

Actual
The real building, as modelled from survey + drawings.
Notional
The Part L compliance target benchmark.
Reference
The fixed EPC scale anchor — frozen at the 2006 Part L spec.
Typical
Stock-average, derived from the Notional.
Job 1 · BRUKL

Part L compliance

BER vs TER  (from the Notional)

England & Wales also compare primary energy (BPER vs TPER); Scotland uses delivered energy (BDER vs TDER). A new-build / refurb gate.

Job 2 · EPCgen

EPC asset rating

SER = RER × 0.765  ·  AR = BER / SER × 50

Mapped onto the A–G scale, with the B/C border pinned at AR = 50. A sale / lease / build certificate.

The buried quirk. The Reference building is always gas-heated, mixed-mode cooled and naturally ventilated, frozen at 2006 standards. So the A–G scale is anchored to a gas hypothetical — an all-electric building can score worse than a gas one even when its real-world carbon is lower. The headline letter can be structurally misleading.
Both targets are theoretical buildings; the operating patterns are standardised. The EPC is a modelled number from assumptions and drawings — never a meter reading. That "modelled-vs-metered gap" is the deepest unsolved problem in the stack.

Strategic read

What the whole iSBEM stack means for the venture — the locked thesis, plus the two refinements the EPC-generation volume forced.

Position
Sit above, don't compete

Every calculation tier is accredited, hard and low-margin. Consume their files; live in the asset-owner's workflow, not the assessor's calculator.

Channel
The assessor is the on-ramp

The rich data is born at, and stays with, the accredited assessor. The relationship to that channel is the route to audit-grade data.

Difficulty
The moat is collection, not code

The unautomatable work is gathering and judging data — survey, inference, zoning. That's where defensibility lives, not in the software.

The atom
The EPC is the reused data point

One modelled number flows into MEES legality, UKSRS disclosure, GRESB, lender covenants and valuation. Own its workflow, sit at the centre.

Refinement · moat
Two on-ramps, one gap

The public register is a thin projection of a lodged XML. The rich model stays private in the .nct. The gap between them — not the public data — is the asset.

Refinement · scope
A second asset class

Each run also emits a coded, payback-banded recommendations set and a (Scotland) scenario engine — a retrofit-planning dataset sitting next to the rating, ready for MEES & stranding triage.
